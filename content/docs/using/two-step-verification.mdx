title: Two-step verification
description: Protect an email-and-password account with an authenticator app, recovery codes or passkeys, how to sign in with each, and what to do if you lose your phone.
---

Two-step verification protects your account if someone learns your password. After the password you also give something only you have: a code from an authenticator app on your phone, or a passkey. It is under **Settings → Account**, in the **Two-step verification** section.

It is for accounts that sign in with an email address and a password. If you sign in through your organisation, the section says **Managed by your organisation**: your organisation's own sign-in decides what it asks of you, and there is nothing to set up here. Open Chat Interface (OCI) never holds a person who signs in that way to a requirement.

## Setting up an authenticator app

You need an authenticator app: any app that supports time-based one-time codes, such as your phone's password manager or an app like Aegis, 2FAS or Microsoft Authenticator.

<Steps>
<Step>
In **Settings → Account**, under **Two-step verification**, select **Set up an authenticator app** and enter your password.
</Step>
<Step>
Scan the QR code with the app. If you cannot scan it, type the key shown under it into the app (it can be selected and copied), or open the link on your phone.
</Step>
<Step>
Type the six-digit code the app shows now and confirm.
</Step>
<Step>
**Save your recovery codes.** You are shown ten, once. Copy, download or print them and keep them somewhere other than the phone.
</Step>
</Steps>

You are signed in again as you finish, so the device you are on carries on without asking.

## Recovery codes

Each of the ten recovery codes works once, to sign in if you cannot use your authenticator app. They are not shown again; the page only says how many are left.

**Get new recovery codes** needs your password and a current code. It replaces the whole set, so the old codes stop working. Use it when you have used several, or if you think someone saw them.

If you sign in with a recovery code, the page tells you how many are left, and you are emailed if your administrator has set up email. Make a new set before you run out.

## Passkeys

A passkey is a key kept on your device or in your password manager, which you unlock with your fingerprint, face or device PIN. It cannot be typed into a fake website, so it is the strongest option where your devices support it. A passkey is a second step on its own: it needs no password and no code.

- **Add a passkey** from the same section. You must have signed in recently; if not, you are asked to sign out, enter your password and sign in again first.
- A passkey must verify you with a PIN, fingerprint or face. A security key that only checks that it was touched is refused, because it would be one factor pretending to be two.
- Passkeys are listed with a name, when you added them, and whether they are kept on one device only or synced across your devices. You can **Rename** and **Remove** them.
- A passkey belongs to the web address of your OCI instance. If your administrator moves the instance to a new address, the passkeys stop working there; your password and authenticator still do.

If your browser cannot make passkeys, the page says so.

## Signing in

### With an authenticator app

Enter your email address and password as usual. You are then asked for an **Authentication code**:

- Type the six digits from your app. A space in the middle is fine, and you can paste it.
- Or select **Use a recovery code instead** and type one of your recovery codes. Capital and small letters are different; the hyphen can be left out.

A code works once: after you use it, wait for the next code the app shows. Five wrong codes end the attempt and you choose **Start again**. Ten wrong codes in a row lock the second step for 15 minutes.

### With a passkey

On the sign-in page, select **Use a passkey** and confirm with your fingerprint, face or device PIN. You do not enter a password or a code. If you have started with your password, **Use a passkey instead** is offered on the code page.

There is no "remember this device": a shared computer is asked every time.

## If you lose your phone

- **You have your recovery codes.** Sign in with your email and password, select **Use a recovery code instead** and enter one. Then, in **Settings → Account**, select **Get new recovery codes**. To use a new phone, select **Turn off** (a recovery code is accepted there) and set up the authenticator again.
- **You have a passkey on another device.** Select **Use a passkey** on the sign-in page.
- **You have neither.** Ask an administrator to reset your two-step verification. It removes your authenticator, recovery codes and passkeys and signs you out everywhere. You can then sign in with your password and set it up again. You are emailed when this happens, if email is set up.

Authenticator codes depend on the time on your phone. If correct codes are refused, check that the phone's date and time are set automatically.

## Turning it off

**Turn off**, in the authenticator row, asks for your password and a current code (an authenticator code or a recovery code), so that someone at an unlocked screen cannot remove it. Your passkeys stay until you remove them.

If your administrator requires two-step verification for your role, you cannot go without it: you are asked to set it up again.

## When your administrator requires it

An administrator can require two-step verification for a role, with a grace period (14 days by default). See [Identity and access](/docs/administration/identity).

- **Before the deadline**, a banner reminds you: "Set up two-step verification by *date*", with **Set up now**. **Settings → Account** says "Required by your administrator. Set it up by *date*."
- **After the deadline**, until you set it up you can reach only the setup page and **Sign out**. Any other page shows that notice.
- **Setting it up** lifts the limit straight away. So does signing in with a passkey.

If you already have two-step verification but your session was opened with your password only, the page asks you to sign out and sign in again, then enter your code or use your passkey.

## What is recorded

Setting it up, turning it off, new recovery codes, passkeys, wrong codes and resets are recorded in the audit log, never the codes or the key themselves. You are emailed about changes when your administrator has set up email.
