## The sidebar

The sidebar lists your [projects](/docs/using/projects), each with its own conversations, then your other conversations under **Pinned**, **Today**, **Yesterday** and **Older**.

<Screenshot slot="sidebar-projects" />

- Under **Projects**, each project has an arrow that shows or hides its five most recent conversations; select its name to open the project page. **Show all (*n*)** opens the project's conversations. Projects start collapsed, and whether each is open is remembered in this browser and kept in step across its open tabs. The project you are working in opens by itself while you are there.
- A conversation in a project is listed under its project, not in the date groups.
- **Pinned** lists every pinned conversation, including project ones, which show their project's name after the title.

On a phone, open the sidebar from the top left.

## Pinning, renaming and archiving

Point at a conversation in the sidebar (or move to it with Tab) to show its buttons:

- **Pin** keeps it at the top, under **Pinned**. **Unpin** puts it back.
- **Rename** (the pencil): see [Renaming a conversation](#renaming-a-conversation).
- **Archive** removes it from the sidebar without deleting it. Archived conversations are under [Settings → History](/docs/using/settings#history) → **Archived**, where **Restore** brings one back. Search still finds them.

## Renaming a conversation

A conversation is named automatically from your first message. To change the name, point at it in the sidebar and choose the pencil (**Rename thread**), or, with the conversation open, choose the pencil at the top right (**Rename conversation**). Type the new name and press Enter to save, or Escape to leave it as it was.

Names are 1 to 200 characters; spaces at either end are dropped. The new name shows straight away in the sidebar, in search and in [Settings → History](/docs/using/settings#history).

## The trash

Delete conversations from [Settings → History](/docs/using/settings#history): tick them and select **Delete**. They move to the **Trash** tab, where each shows when it will be deleted for good.

- **Restore** brings a conversation back (with its files, if you have room for them in your storage allowance). Share links it had stay revoked.
- **Delete now** deletes one permanently; **Empty trash** deletes them all. This cannot be undone.

How long the trash keeps things, and whether old conversations are moved there automatically, is set by your institution. A conversation removed for inactivity is marked "removed automatically".

## Finding a conversation

Type in the sidebar's search box, or press `Cmd/Ctrl + K`, to search your conversations. Results show each conversation's title with its best-matching lines underneath, matched words highlighted, best match first.

<Screenshot slot="conversation-search" />

- Every word you type must appear, and each matches the start of a word: `migr plan` finds "migration planning". Punctuation is ignored.
- Capitals and accents do not matter, in titles and in the text of messages: `bibliotheque` finds "bibliothèque", `busqueda` finds "Búsqueda", and `strasse` finds "Straße". This applies to Latin letters; words in other scripts match as written. After an upgrade to v0.13, older conversations can take a while to be searched this way (your administrator can see the progress); until then accents in message text still matter. Search of a [project's files](/docs/using/projects) ignores accents too.
- Search covers titles, your messages and the replies you were shown. It does not search reasoning, web sources or the contents of attached files.
- Archived conversations are included and marked **Archived**; project conversations show their project. Conversations in the trash and temporary chats are not included.
- Choosing a result opens the conversation at the matching message, briefly highlighted, instead of at the end. **Jump to latest** takes you to the end.

`Cmd/Ctrl + K` also opens commands: a new chat, your history, models, attachments, settings, switching theme, and (for administrators and auditors) every admin page.

## Very long conversations

From v0.11 a conversation opens at its **latest 100 messages**. Earlier ones load in parts as you scroll up, without moving what you are reading; **Load earlier messages** at the top does the same from the keyboard, and a screen reader hears how many were loaded. Opening a conversation from search loads the messages around the match and the latest ones; **Load more messages** fills in between, and scrolling towards the gap fills it too.

Once a conversation is long, only the messages near what you are looking at are drawn on the page, so even thousands of messages scroll smoothly and replies stream as before.

<Callout title="Your browser's Find only finds what is drawn">
  **Ctrl+F** (or **Cmd+F**) only finds messages that are drawn, which in a long conversation means those near the view. To find something anywhere in a conversation, use [conversation search](#finding-a-conversation) (the sidebar's search box, or `Cmd/Ctrl + K`): it searches every message and opens the conversation at the match.
</Callout>

Exports and share links always contain the whole conversation.

## Data classes

If your institution has set up [data classification](/docs/administration/data-classification), a conversation can carry **data classes**: names such as "Student records" for the kind of data it holds. They appear as chips under the top of the conversation, each with the reason it is there:

- **from the project**: the conversation is in a [project](/docs/using/projects#data-classes-on-a-project) labelled with the class. A label an administrator set says **set by an administrator**.
- **from the *name* connector**: the conversation has used that [connector](/docs/using/tools-and-connectors), whose results carry the class. Once used, always: the conversation holds that data from then on.
- **added by you**: you labelled it with **Add label**. Only these have a remove button (an **×**).
- **kept from its earlier project**, or **kept from the removed *name* connector**: the class came with the conversation when it was moved out of a project, or the project or connector was deleted. A label is never lost as a side effect, and forking a conversation copies its labels, so these cannot be removed here.

If you are not sure why a label cannot be removed, the page says why.

### Models that are not approved

Your administrator approves each model for the classes it may receive. In the model picker, a model that is not approved for a class your conversation carries stays in the list, greyed out, with **Not approved for *class*** as text. You cannot choose it, but it can still be reached with the arrow keys, and a screen reader reads the reason. A new chat in a labelled project starts on an approved model. If the model you are on is not approved, a note above the message box says so before you send anything.

### A refused message

If a message is refused anyway, for example because a label was added in another tab, it is **not sent**. Its text goes back into the message box, and the page names the class that is missing and lists the approved models as **Use *model*** buttons. Choose one and send the same text again.

While your institution is trying the feature out (**Warn** mode), nothing is refused. The message is sent, a note above the reply says the conversation carries a class the model is not approved for, and your administrator can see it.

### What else a label changes

- A label does not recall a message already sent: if you label a conversation after it went to a model that is not approved, the next message is refused, and the earlier one is not taken back.
- Memory notes are never written from a conversation that carries a class.
- In **Block** mode, [share links](/docs/using/sharing#conversations-with-data-classes) cannot be made for the conversation, and one made earlier is paused while it is labelled.
- Labels are not part of a downloaded export.

## Downloading one conversation

The download button at the top right of a conversation saves it as Markdown. To save everything at once, see [Your data](/docs/using/your-data).

## Temporary chats

A temporary chat stays out of the sidebar and search, and expires. See [Chatting](/docs/using/chat#temporary-chats).
