---
title: Groups
description: What being in a group changes for you, and how a group manager adds and removes the people in the groups they manage.
---

A group is a set of people your institution has put together, such as a department or a course. Being in a group can give you a model or a usage limit that others do not have. It never takes anything away from you.

You do not need to do anything to be in a group: an administrator or a group manager adds you. Administrators set up groups; see [Groups](/docs/administration/groups).

## If you are in a group

A group can add two things to what your role already gives you:

- **Models.** A group can grant models that your role does not offer. They appear in the model picker like any other.
- **Usage budgets.** A group can carry budgets. When several budgets apply to you, the most restrictive one applies.

A group only adds. It never removes a model or a feature your role gives you. Your usage limits keep their usual wording, such as "You have reached your message limit for today"; they do not name the group.

A group can also be used to share a project with everyone in it. See [Sharing a project](/docs/using/projects#sharing-a-project).

When you are added or removed, the change applies from your next request. If a model a group grants is not showing yet, reload the page.

## If you manage a group

A manager decides who is in the group. If you manage at least one group, **Settings → Groups** appears; it does not appear for anyone else. It lists the groups you manage, with how many people are in each and how many more it can hold (a group has at most 5,000 members).

You can:

- **See who is in the group**, with names, email addresses and whether each is a member or a manager. Search by name or email.
- **Add people** by email address, up to 200 at a time, one per line or separated by commas. Each address ends up in one of three lists: added, already in the group, or no account with that address. Only people who already have an account can be added, and people you add are members, not managers.
- **Remove a member.** It takes effect straight away: the models and limits the group gave them stop applying on their next message. They keep their account, role and conversations.
- **Leave** the group yourself. You then stop managing it, and an administrator can add you back.

You cannot:

- see what the group grants (its budgets and models), or its usage;
- rename or delete the group;
- make someone a manager or remove a manager;
- look at a group you do not manage.

Ask an administrator for those. An administrator appoints managers, up to 20 for a group.

Looking up email addresses is limited to 30 requests a minute, to stop people guessing who has an account. Every change you make is recorded for administrators, with your name.

<Callout title="Someone has no account yet">
  You cannot add a person who has no account. Ask them to sign in once, or ask an administrator to invite them.
</Callout>
